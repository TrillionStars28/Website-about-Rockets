Have you ever been curious of what a Spaceship/Rocketship really is?

Well look no further. Spaceships and Rocketships have a lot in common, but work a lot differently than normal things that fly through the sky.

Forces and How they Work

For example, your generic airplane uses a combination of 4 forces to stay in flight. These four forces being Thrust, Drag, Weight, and Lift. Using a combination of these forces, an airplane can stay flying through the sky. Using thrust to generate, well, thrust. Which moves the aircraft foward. While also utilizing lift to generate upwards movement, it does this through the big wings you commonly see attached to the aircraft. Weight and drag are the counter acting forces of thrust and lift. While drag reduces thrust, and weight reduces lift. These forces are very important to take into account, for too much of either one can keep an aircraft from flying. And prevent proper flight of your spaceship/rocketship.

Too Much Force

Some examples of too much of a force doing this, is having too much weight, being a very common force to have too much of preventing the flight of an aircraft. For when you have too much weight, you require much more lift to maintain upwards momentum, and much more initial thrust to set the aircraft into motion.

Another example, is when you have too much drag. When an aircraft has too much drag, it requires a lot more thrust in order to get into motion, and to stay in motion, meaning you need constant thrust to counteract it. This becomes a problem when you require more thrust than the engines on your aircraft are able to provide, leading to midair stalling, and prevention of takeoff.

In general, it's important to have an equal and balanced distribution of these forces for optimal flight performance. Or else your aircraft may not be able to achieve or maintain flight as planned to, which isn't ideal for when your wanting to fly with it.

How do Spaceships and Rocketships get affected by these Forces?

Spaceships and Rocketships are affected by these forces in a very similar manner to how an airplane is affected by them. However, they have a much more complex relationship with these forces, as they have to take into account the fact that they are flying through space, and not just through the atmosphere.

Rocketship Forces!

The four forces behind the whole shabang, and how they directly interact with eachother during flight.

Thrust

First force to consider is Thrust, the power force which provides sufficient propulsion for the rocket to overcome gravity (Weight) and atmospheric Drag. Without Thrust, the rocket would be unable to propel itself foward, or anywhere for that matter. Making thrust an essential force for flight through the atmosphere. It's also required in order to provie movement, and spontaneous momentum in space. Now, you might be wondering, "Well how does that work, engines dont work in space?". While that is true, that's because those are normal engines which require oxygen to preform their combustion cycles. But spacecraft dont use normal engines, and instead use a combination of liquid fuel and solid fuel propellents, and boosters, which dont require direct oxygen in the atmosphere to function. You'll akso see some thrusters use a concentrated stream of gas or air, which is what a normal big thruster on the bottom of a rocket does, just in a much smaller and precise manner.

Lift

Second force to consider is Lift, the force that allows for a rocket to stay in the air and maintain stability on its course to space. Lift directly counter acts the force of weight that a rocket would experience, and provides necessary stability while flying through the atmosphere. Lift and thrust go hand and hand, for thrust can generate both foward and upward momentum. While lift can generate both upward and foward momentum aswell.

Drag

Third force to consider is Drag, it's the resistance force that opposes the motion off the rocket thorugh its course in atmosphere. Drag is caused by the fricion and compression of air molecules in the atmosphere, which is why you experience almost zero drag in space. But that doesn't mean Drag isn't important to take into account for a rocket. Since Drag can greatly increase the force needed to take off and maintain flight. Meaning the rocket has to work harder to counter act it, in turn burning more fuel or requiring stronger thrusters. Which could all be avoided if the rocket is made aerodynamically efficient, greatly reducing Drag.

Weight

The last force to consider, and arguably most important, is Weight. The one force that directly impacts all of the other forces, and is the most stubborn one to balance. Due to this, it makes it one of the most challenging forces to manage in rocket design, since the more Weight you have the more thrust and lift you require to overcome the inertia of the Weight. But not onlt does it need additional forces to counteract, it also influences the amount of drag you experience. Meaning you'll have to balance most of your forcec around weight.
